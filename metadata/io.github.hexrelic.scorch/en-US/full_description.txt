Scorch overwrites files before deleting them, wipes the free space other apps left behind, and strips hidden metadata from photos. It works entirely offline.

<b>What it does</b>
• Shreds files and folders: overwritten in place pass by pass, checked, renamed, truncated and deleted
• Five overwrite schedules, labelled honestly (on a phone, one random pass is the sensible choice)
• Wipes free space, overwriting what's left of files other apps deleted
• Removes EXIF (GPS included), XMP, comments and hidden extra pictures from JPEG and PNG, without re-encoding and keeping orientation and colours
• "Shred with Scorch" in the Open with and Share menus of other apps
• Previews and file type icons, so you see exactly what you are about to destroy

<b>Privacy</b>
• No network permission, no trackers, no Play Services
• Fingerprint or screen lock check and a typed SHRED before every job
• Screenshots blocked by default

<b>Limits</b>
Phones store data on flash memory, and no app can guarantee the old bytes are physically gone. If something must never be recovered, keep it encrypted from the start; a factory reset is the strongest wipe a phone offers. Scorch doesn't protect 100% against advanced forensic techniques, and every pass adds wear to the flash. The app explains this in About.

Scorch needs the "All files access" permission. The app is in English.

Free and open-source software (GPL-3.0-or-later), built reproducibly.
